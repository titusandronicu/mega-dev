## Findings
- Signals come from many places, not only logs: webhooks, heartbeats, call and meeting transcripts, support channels, and the other teams (product, marketing, sales), who can turn a signal into a feature request. We have no list of signal sources for our own products (the lesson map, the NAS mail-agent), so nothing says what would tell us a change came back. [evidence: lesson "Signals", figures 1 and 2; `docs/tools.md` lists tools, not signal sources] [fix: playbook] [status: proposed] [idea: write-down-state, package-it]
- A bug report can go all the way round without a person: signal, triage, agreement, cloud agent, proof, merge, reply, and the source must be trusted or a prompt can be injected through it. Our S03E02 triage stops at the issue; nothing replies to the source after the merge. [evidence: lesson "Signals", figure 4 and the note on trusted sources; `challenges/S03E02/log.md`] [fix: skill] [status: proposed] [idea: package-it, distrust-decoys]
- Observing a released feature needs an agreement and evidence to read back, then an instruction, skills, a trigger, a first run that proves the setup, a note after each run, and permission for the agent to turn itself off. The only run log we keep is the mail-agent journal (S02E05); a merged change gets no observation. [evidence: lesson "Observation", figures 5 and 6; memory note `s02e05-while-youre-away-lessons` (journal)] [fix: playbook] [status: proposed] [idea: write-down-state, prove-after-merge]
- Synthesis is not a summary: cluster signals, link a returning issue to the earlier fix, and fix the root cause before a quick fix, without undoing a recorded decision. Kent's example was a Sentry issue that came back in a new form; the agent linked it to the old one and waited for approval. Our retros link findings to lessons by hand; nothing links a new failure to an earlier one. [evidence: lesson "Synthesis", figures 7 and 8; `docs/lessons-index.md` (manual map)] [fix: skill] [status: proposed] [idea: source-over-summary, look-again]
- Decisions need four settings: barriers the agent cannot override, an agreement that says when to contact the owner, reversibility, and the owner's contact preferences by urgency. `CLAUDE.md` "Merging, by risk" covers barriers and some reversibility; it has no contact rule or channel preference. [evidence: lesson "Decisions"; `CLAUDE.md` "Merging, by risk"] [fix: claude-md] [status: proposed] [idea: human-at-fork, code-over-prompt]
- After every activity ask one question: did anything useful come out, and where should it go? The lesson names five places: issues, specifications, evaluations, product memory, agent context. Our retros do it once per Challenge, by hand; no activity (a review, a scheduled run, a call) asks it. [evidence: lesson "Feedback Loops", figure 9] [fix: playbook] [status: proposed] [idea: write-down-state, package-it]
- A friction log with a daily sweep closes the loop: a repeat obstacle becomes an issue labelled friction, a daily job fixes it or parks it with a recommended fix, and the loop closes only when the original signal goes quiet. Deleting matters as much as adding, or the context rots. We keep `docs/didnt-work.md` as a list; nothing sweeps it, merges its repeats or deletes what a tool now prevents. [evidence: lesson "Feedback Loops" (friction log, cleanup after migration); `docs/didnt-work.md`] [fix: tool] [status: proposed] [idea: look-again, code-over-prompt]
