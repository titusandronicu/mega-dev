## Findings
- Match the evidence to the risk and the kind of change. The lesson sorts changes into composes (small fix), extends (changes behavior) and one-way door (new primitive, data, money), and gives each more kinds of proof: checks, captured proof, a separate reviewer, a person. Our "Merging, by risk" lists three classes but not what evidence each needs. [evidence: lesson "Acceptance", figure 1; `CLAUDE.md` "Merging, by risk"] [fix: claude-md] [status: proposed] [idea: contract-first, human-at-fork]
- "Agent says done" is not acceptance, and evidence can be generated instead of captured. Every criterion in the agreement keeps its row until captured evidence is attached; one with no proof is marked "missing tool", and scope changes during the work are reported, not silent. Our rule asks for a named check but not a per-criterion table in the PR. [evidence: lesson "Acceptance", figure 2; `CLAUDE.md` "name the check you ran"] [fix: playbook] [status: proposed] [idea: source-over-summary, distrust-decoys]
- Build a lab for agents only: scripts that start and drive the real thing, save screenshots and logs to one folder, give each run its own account, fake the calls that cost money, and block what cannot be undone. Our `rehearsal/mock.mjs` (S03E03) is a small version of this for one Challenge; nothing is shared or kept as a lab. [evidence: lesson "Verification", figure 3 and the list of limits; `challenges/S03E03/rehearsal/`] [fix: tool] [status: proposed] [idea: rehearse-the-shape, code-over-prompt]
- Routines should read the job history after each job and once a week, then propose merging, retiring and scripting tools in a pull request. The agent must also keep a list of which routines are active and where they were started. We have no routine and no list of them. [evidence: lesson "Verification", figure 4 and "keep track of which routines are active"; no scheduled routine in `.claude/` or `docs/`] [fix: playbook] [status: proposed] [idea: write-down-state, look-again]
- A system diff is a page made for the person, not the agent's summary: answer first, then decisions with a recommended option, then map, before and after, and files on click. The owner chooses purpose, format and content; the agents scan recent PRs and our history and write the first version of the skill; "what it missed" flows back. We have no such skill; PRs carry the diff only. [evidence: lesson "System Diff", figures 5 and 6] [fix: skill] [status: proposed] [idea: package-it, human-at-fork]
- Review rounds need principles, sources and boundaries, or reviewers push too hard, not hard enough, or undo each other. Examples are a round limit, "one-way door goes to a person" and "a note, not an edit, when another agent owns the file". Our `.claude/agents/reviewer.md` reviews in a fresh context but has none of these boundaries or a last round. [evidence: lesson "Review", figure 7; `.claude/agents/reviewer.md`] [fix: skill] [status: proposed] [idea: human-at-fork, distrust-decoys]
- Quality gates are rules, not principles, and they stay deterministic. A comment that a reviewer makes twice becomes a house rule in the gate, and a nightly agent removes weak tests so the gate stays fast. `tools/check-all` is already the one gate; it has no step that turns a repeated review comment into a check. [evidence: lesson "Quality Gates", figure 8; `tools/check-all`; transcript "pre-merge gates"] [fix: tool] [status: proposed] [idea: code-over-prompt, package-it]
- Merged is not shipped. After a merge the agent subscribes to the deploy job, then asks the health endpoint which commit is live, and fixes the job if it is not the one it made. The lesson-map page is the case here: `update.sh` pulls main and nothing says which commit the page shows. This change stamps the build commit on the page and in `/version.json`. [evidence: transcript "it's going to hit the health endpoint"; `docs/tools.md` lesson-map row (`update.sh` runs `git pull`)] [fix: tool] [status: proposed] [idea: prove-after-merge, code-over-prompt]
- Put a risky change behind a flag so a one-way door becomes a two-way door: off by default, on for people who opted in, a success metric tracked, deleted once everyone has it. Kent's example: 211 packages needed migration, found while the flag was only on for eager users. We ship by deploy only and have no flag. [evidence: transcript "credential need enforcement flag... off by default"; no flag mechanism in this repo] [fix: playbook] [status: proposed] [idea: prove-after-merge, human-at-fork]

We take a tar before editing the NAS `mail-agent`, but I see no restore drill and the tars sit on the same NAS as the volumes.
